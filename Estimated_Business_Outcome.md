**HR ANALYTICS DASHBOARD — ESTIMATED BUSINESS IMPACT**

Dataset: Employee\_Performance\_Data.csv (10,000 employees)

=====================================================



**METHOD:**



\- Correlated Performance\_Score against tenure, salary, training hours,

&#x20; attendance, work hours, and promotion history.

\- Isolated the bottom 10% of performers by Performance\_Score and compared

&#x20; their profile (training, satisfaction, attendance, pay) against the rest

&#x20; of the workforce to find the levers most associated with underperformance.



**KEY FINDINGS:**



1\. Job satisfaction is the single strongest driver of performance

&#x20;  (correlation r = 0.86), well ahead of salary (r = 0.76), promotion history

&#x20;  (r = 0.76), and tenure (r = 0.46).



2\. Training has a clear, monotonic effect on performance: employees in the

&#x20;  top quartile of training hours (60-79 hrs) average a Performance\_Score of

&#x20;  93.6, vs. 84.2 for the bottom quartile (0-19 hrs) — an 11% performance

&#x20;  gap explained by training investment alone.



3\. Work hours per week show almost no relationship with performance

&#x20;  (r = 0.007) or attendance — i.e., employees working longer hours are not

&#x20;  measurably more productive in this dataset, a useful data point against

&#x20;  an "hours = output" culture.



4\. Promotions appear largely merit-based: promoted employees average a

&#x20;  Performance\_Score of 94.4 vs. 65.1 for non-promoted employees — a strong

&#x20;  signal that the promotion process is rewarding real performance rather

&#x20;  than tenure or other factors.



5\. Bottom 10% performers (Performance\_Score <= 65.1, n=1,000) look

&#x20;  structurally different from the rest of the workforce:

&#x20;    - 32% less training: 27.6 hrs vs. 40.4 hrs for everyone else.

&#x20;    - 38% lower job satisfaction: 5.55 vs. 9.02 (on a 10-point scale).

&#x20;    - Lower attendance: 82.5% vs. 85.3%.

&#x20;    - Much lower pay: $50,829 vs. $122,848 average monthly salary

&#x20;      (likely reflects role/seniority mix, not just performance).



6\. Department-level performance is fairly uniform (88.2-89.6 average score

&#x20;  across all 7 departments) — underperformance in this data is an

&#x20;  individual/engagement issue, not a department-specific problem.



**ESTIMATED BUSINESS OUTCOME:**



\- Because training hours and performance move together in a clear,

&#x20; stepwise pattern, bringing the bottom**-10%** performer group's training

&#x20; exposure up from **27.6 hrs** to the workforce average (**40.4 hrs**) would be

&#x20; expected to close a meaningful share of their 11%-scale performance gap —

&#x20; a realistic, low-cost lever compared to compensation changes.



\- Given job satisfaction is the top correlate of performance **(r=0.86),**

&#x20; even a modest, targeted engagement intervention for the bottom**-10%**

&#x20; performer group (whose satisfaction is **38% below average**) is likely to

&#x20; have an outsized impact on overall workforce performance relative to

&#x20; its cost.
